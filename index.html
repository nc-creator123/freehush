Removing background noise for free: the whole map

Searches for free noise cancellation split into two very different problems, and half the frustration out there comes from applying the wrong kind of tool. The first problem is live audio — meetings, calls, streams — where the noise has to be removed in real time, before anyone hears it. The second is recorded audio — a podcast episode, an interview, a voice memo — where you want to remove background noise from audio online free, after the fact, usually by uploading a file and downloading a cleaner one. Every tool on this site belongs firmly to one camp, and we label which.

For live calls and streams

The free options are stronger than most people expect. If your PC has a GeForce RTX card, NVIDIA Broadcast gives unlimited noise removal at no cost. On any other Windows or Mac machine, start with the suppression already built into the meeting app: Zoom and Teams switch theirs on by default, Discord runs Krisp's filter free inside Discord, and Google Meet's noise cancellation comes with Workspace editions and Google One 2 TB plans (plus a device-based version for everyone on most Android phones). Tuned once, those handle steady noise like fans and traffic surprisingly well. Krisp's own app — one filter for every app — is now paid after a 7-day trial; our Krisp page has the prices. Streamers get a capable filter free inside OBS. The full ranking lives in our best free desktop noise cancellation roundup.

For recordings you already have

To clean up a noisy recording without installing anything, Adobe Podcast Enhance processes an hour a day free in the browser and can rescue genuinely rough audio, while Auphonic gives two free hours a month of proper podcast post-production — noise reduction, levelling and loudness in one pass. Prefer offline and unlimited? Audacity's noise reduction has been quietly excellent at constant hiss and hum for two decades. Quick questions

How can I remove background noise from audio online, free?

Upload it to Adobe Podcast Enhance (free hour daily, files up to 30 minutes) for dramatic voice cleanup, or to Auphonic (two free hours monthly) for more faithful, podcast-grade processing. Both are browser-only — no installs. Our online removers roundup compares them side by side.

What's the best free noise cancellation for meetings?

With a GeForce RTX GPU: NVIDIA Broadcast — unlimited and free. Without one: your meeting app's own suppression — on by default in Zoom and Teams, Krisp-powered and free inside Discord. Krisp's standalone app no longer has a free plan (a 7-day trial, then paid). The desktop roundup ranks all the options.

Is any noise cancellation software completely free — no limits at all?

Yes, four kinds: the filters inside Zoom, Teams and Discord (unlimited, but each works only in its own app), NVIDIA Broadcast (unlimited, but needs RTX hardware), OBS's built-in filter (unlimited, but only inside OBS), and Audacity (unlimited, but for recordings rather than live calls). The two metered picks — Adobe and Auphonic — have their exact allowance printed on their cards above.

Do free noise removers add watermarks?

The ones we list don't watermark your audio — with one disclosed exception: Auphonic marks free productions with a jingle, which we state plainly in its review. The wider category is messier; several popular web editors process your file free and then watermark the export. Our roundup includes the ambush patterns to check before uploading anything.

Is free noise cancellation as good as paid?

Against steady noise — fans, hum, traffic — usually yes. Paid tools earn their money in three specific places: unlimited use when free minutes run out, harder cases like background voices, and cleaning incoming audio too. If you're not hitting one of those walls weekly, stay free. The full logic is in free vs paid.

Is there a completely free noise cancelling app for my phone?

Not as a standalone app — but the meeting apps carry their own. Zoom and Teams list noise suppression for their Android and iOS apps, Discord's Krisp filter works in its mobile app, and Google Meet runs device-based noise cancellation on most Android phones. For recordings, upload to the online removers from your mobile browser.

What's the catch with free noise cancellation software?

Never money — always a stated cap: Enhance caps file length, Auphonic counts monthly hours, Broadcast demands an RTX card, the built-in filters stay inside their own app, Audacity charges patience. We print each cap on the card because a limit you can plan around is a fair deal, and a hidden one is a trap.
