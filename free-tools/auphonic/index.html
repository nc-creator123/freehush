How it holds up

From Auphonic's documentation — this is a fourteen-year-old service with unusually transparent docs:

For podcast episodes and interviews: the target use, and where it shines. The value isn't one spectacular algorithm — it's the chain: denoise plus per-speaker levelling plus loudness normalisation to platform standards, decisions most hobbyists get wrong by hand. Its noise reduction also lets you choose whether to treat only static noise or faster-changing noise, and whether music should survive.

For quick one-off cleanups: workable but heavyweight — the upload-configure-download loop is built for episodes, not voice memos. Simpler web tools feel faster for a 90-second file (and mind the 3-minute billing minimum).

What to check on your own audio: whether the jingle marking on free productions is acceptable for your use; how its noise reduction compares to Adobe Enhance on your voice (Auphonic subtracts, Adobe rebuilds — they fail in opposite directions); and whether 2 hours actually covers your month.

The catch: two hours a month sounds generous until you produce weekly — four 40-minute episodes and you're already over. The free tier is a real trial-of-lifestyle, not a production plan for regulars.

Running your first production

  1. Create a free account at auphonic.com.
  2. Click New Production and upload your file.
  3. Enable Noise and Hum Reduction (Auto is a sane start) and leave Adaptive Leveler and loudness target on.
  4. Start the production, then listen to the result next to your original — including how the free-tier marking sounds.
  5. Download, and note your remaining free hours on the dashboard.

When you'll outgrow it

  • You publish more than ~2 hours monthly — the direct wall. €12 a month (€10 billed yearly) buys 9 hours; occasional producers do better with the never-expiring one-time credit packs.
  • The jingle marking doesn't fit your output — paid removes it.
  • Your problem is live calls — wrong tool family entirely; see the desktop options.

What two free hours a month actually covers

Auphonic's meter is monthly, not daily — which changes the strategy completely from Enhance's. Two hours covers: a weekly show of 25–30 minute episodes with nothing to spare, a fortnightly hour-long show comfortably, or one long interview plus change. The hours don't roll over, so an unused month is a spent month — batch your processing near month-end if your schedule is lumpy.

Auphonic vs Enhance: polish vs rescue

They look like rivals and mostly aren't. Adobe's Enhance is a rescue tool — it rebuilds damaged speech, dramatically, and gives you an hour a day to do it. Auphonic is a finishing tool: loudness normalization to podcast standards, leveling between voices, and noise reduction that's deliberately gentler than a rebuild. Feed a genuinely wrecked recording to Auphonic and you'll wish you'd used Enhance; feed a decent recording to Enhance and you may get an over-polished sheen where Auphonic would have delivered broadcast-ready. Damaged audio → Enhance first. Decent audio that needs to sound professional → Auphonic. Both free lanes are compared head-to-head in the online noise removers roundup.

Quick questions

Do unused free hours carry over to next month?

No — the 2 free hours reset monthly and don't accumulate. If your production schedule is bursty, Auphonic's one-time credit packs (which never expire) fit better than the subscription.

Is Auphonic's free tier feature-limited?

No — that's its distinguishing move. You get the full algorithm chain on free; the tier is limited by hours (and the production marking), not by capability. It's a genuine test of the real product.

Auphonic or Adobe Podcast Enhance?

Different philosophies: Auphonic subtracts noise and engineers levels while keeping your voice yours; Adobe rebuilds the speech and can sound more transformed — sometimes better, sometimes artificial. Run the same rough file through both free tiers and trust your ears.
