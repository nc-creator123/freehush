How the free tier holds up

We build these assessments from Krisp's documentation plus the shape of the limits — and we'll tell you exactly what to test on your own calls:

For a daily stand-up plus the odd call: comfortably inside the allowance. The cancellation applies both ways — your mic out, their noise in — which is something no in-app suppression gives you, and it's the whole reason to bother with a system-level tool on a free tier.

For back-to-back meeting days: the math stops working. Three hours of calls against a 60–90 minute allowance means choosing which meetings deserve the clean audio — a genuinely annoying mode of operation that the upgrade exists to end.

What to check yourself in the first week: whether the minutes counter's pace fits your calendar; how it behaves on your quietest device (the processing is real work for old laptops); and whether the single-device rule bites — if you bounce between a desktop and a laptop, the sign-out dance gets old.

The catch: minutes don't roll over — unused allowance dies at midnight GMT. Sixty free minutes daily is generous until the one day you have a two-hour workshop.

Setting it up

  1. Download Krisp for Windows or Mac from krisp.ai and install it.
  2. Create the free account — use your work email if you have one, for the 1.5× daily minutes.
  3. In your meeting app's audio settings, select Krisp Microphone as the mic (and Krisp Speaker for incoming cleanup).
  4. Toggle noise cancellation on from the Krisp widget, and watch the remaining-minutes counter there too.

When you'll outgrow it

The free plan's design tells you who it's for: occasional-meeting people. You've outgrown it when any of these is true —

  • You're rationing minutes across meetings more days than not.
  • You need it on two machines without the sign-out shuffle.
  • You want unlimited AI notes rather than 2/day.

Stretching the 30 free hours

The allowance is metered as a fresh hour a day, so the skill isn't rationing a monthly pool — it's spending each day's hour on the calls where you're actually being judged. Three habits cover most weeks:

  • Toggle off in quiet rooms. The meter counts cancellation-active minutes, not call minutes. The internal catch-up from your silent home office doesn't need Krisp running — save the hour for the café day.
  • Know your reset. The hour refills at midnight GMT, which lands in the US afternoon/evening. A late-day call can sometimes catch a fresh hour that a morning call couldn't — check the clock before assuming you're out.
  • Bridge with what you already own. When the hour dies mid-day, Zoom's own Noise removal (Settings → Audio, levels from Auto to High) and Teams' suppression toggle hold the line free until the reset — the free desktop noise cancellation roundup ranks every built-in.

Krisp free vs the suppression already in your meeting app

Before assuming you need any install: Zoom ships adjustable noise removal, and Teams' new client keeps a noise suppression switch under Settings → Devices (admins can lock it — if yours is greyed out, that's why). For one app in a mildly noisy room, those toggles are genuinely enough, and they're unlimited.

Krisp free earns its install in three specific situations: your week spans multiple apps and you want one behavior everywhere; you need incoming audio cleaned too (the toggles mostly guard what you send); or the built-in setting is policy-locked by an employer. If none of those is you, keep the toggles and the disk space — the free-vs-paid math says so in bolder type.

Quick questions

Is Krisp Free a trial in disguise?

No — it's a perpetual free plan, and that's exactly why it leads this site. The 7-day trial is a separate, optional thing that previews the paid tier. When the trial ends you fall back to the free plan, not to zero.

Do unused free minutes accumulate?

No. Each day's free hour (90 minutes with a work email) resets at 12:00 AM GMT every day and don't roll over — plan the long calls accordingly.

Does it work with any meeting app?

Yes — Krisp installs as a virtual microphone and speaker, so anything that lets you pick an audio device can use it: Zoom, Teams, Meet, Webex, Discord, Slack huddles, softphones.
